Crucial Physical Inspection: L1 / L2 Terminal Lever Detents

Mandatory Safety Rule: Always shut off the upstream 240V circuit breaker in the main electrical service panel before removing the CPH50 faceplate or opening the wire cover. Never work on live terminals.

Why Wire Levers Cause "Zero Energy Dispensing" (0 kW)

Common Hardware Cause

The ChargePoint Home Flex utilizes heavy-duty spring-loaded lever terminals (WAGO style) for the incoming 240V conductors (L1, L2, and Ground). If the wire lever is pressed down with insufficient force, it may look closed but remain perched just above the mechanical locking detent. Under load, contact resistance climbs, voltage drops precipitously, thermal safety thresholds trip, and the internal contactor opens—resulting in 0 kW energy flow.

Failure Mode: Levers are not pressed down until the audible "CLICK". Wire strands have high impedance contact. When charging begins, thermal sensors detect rapid temperature spike within 30-90 seconds and immediately trip contactors to prevent terminal meltdown.

Step-by-Step Field Inspection Procedure:

  1. De-energize Service: Flip the dedicated 240V dual-pole circuit breaker to the OFF position. Verify 0V with a calibrated digital multimeter at the input.
  2. Remove Faceplate: Gently pull from the bottom of the CPH50 plastic fascia to pop the decorative snaps free.
  3. Open Terminal Cover: Loosen the captive Phillips/Torx retaining screws securing the internal clear/black safety baffle.
  4. Physical Lever Audit: Firmly press each orange/grey lever arm directly downward with thumb pressure until you hear/feel an unmistakable mechanical snap. The top surface of the lever must be perfectly parallel and flush with the terminal casing.
  5. Conductor Pull Test: Give each insulated conductor (L1, L2, GND) a firm tug downwards to confirm the internal clamp has locked onto the bare copper.
  6. Reassemble & Re-energize: Fasten safety cover, snap on fascia, and turn circuit breaker ON. Test a charging cycle.
